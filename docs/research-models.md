# Research model boundary for wing control

For the current cross-experiment results and open questions, see the [research summary](research-results.md).

The [Shiu et al. model](https://github.com/philshiu/Drosophila_brain_model) is a published FlyWire **brain** leaky integrate-and-fire model. Its `model.py` builds Brian2 neurons and synapses, stimulates selected cells with Poisson input, and records spikes. The local source audit used commit `91bdd1e7dcf193f3e7ca5a8933497fcef63b7960`. The [Eon repository](https://github.com/eonsystemspbc/fly-brain), audited at `a3db62f9436074e485c0278290c2164ed6150808`, benchmarks execution backends for the same FlyWire brain model. Neither repository supplies a validated closed-loop quad interface or the VNC wing motor neurons.

[MaleCNS v1.0](https://male-cns.janelia.org/download/) joins brain and ventral nerve cord. Its local annotations include one `b1 MN` and one `b2 MN` per side, plus bilateral `DLMn` and `DVMn` power muscle motor groups. The [wing motor study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9750141/) identifies b1/b2 as flight steering motor units. These names identify genuine anatomical outputs; the repository's `flybrain` dynamics and the conversion from spikes to rotor newtons are separate assumptions.

`fly-fpv wing-direct` is a **MaleCNS/flybrain prototype**, not a Shiu/Eon replication. It feeds camera-derived target bearing to LC10a. DLM/DVM spike counts set left/right total thrust, split between two rotors per side. b1/b2 counts modify side and front/rear distribution. It calibrates resting DLM/DVM counts once so their mean equals 0.75 × 9.81 N, smooths subsequent counts, and applies forces directly. There is no conventional stabilizer in this run. The b1/b2 rotor mapping is an engineering hypothesis; fly wing muscle effects are phase dependent and a quad has different mechanics.

First `seed=7` run: four decisions, 1.23 seconds, then crash. b1/b2 generated zero spikes before the crash. A constant 0.75 × 9.81 / 4 N per rotor holds the same undisturbed quad at 1.2 m for eight seconds, so the immediate fall is from the neural thrust variations, not a broken quad model. See `runs/wing-direct/results.json` for full neuron counts, camera measurements, rotor forces, and pose trace.

An optional `--haltere-feedback` supplies measured roll rate to MaleCNS `SApp08` cells, whose [annotation](https://www.virtualflybrain.org/term/sapp08_l-malecns187182-vfb_jrmc1b7g/) associates them with haltere campaniform sensilla. This is a crude sensor encoder. In the first matched seed it crashed after 0.8 seconds; b1/b2 still produced zero spikes during the flight. The quad camera also supplies only tag bearing, not optic flow. Neither result establishes a flight-capable controller, and one seed does not establish that such feedback cannot help.

The `--retina` diagnostic instead projects horizontally averaged FPV brightness onto annotated photoreceptors. It uses the available azimuth labels and omits vertical receptive fields and true optic flow. With the same seed, it crashed after 1.16 seconds, mainly in roll. The `--altitude-assist` diagnostic adds an external collective thrust correction while leaving neural left/right thrust imbalance intact. It delayed the first crash from 1.23 to 1.41 seconds, but roll exceeded the crash threshold. The raw run commanded 6.37–6.89 N total at its four decisions, below the 7.36 N hover requirement. These results support missing height and attitude feedback as immediate engineering problems; they do not isolate the biological cause of weak wing-cell responses.

## What photoreceptor input represents

`RetinaProjector` converts the middle half of one FPV RGB frame to a horizontal luminance profile and samples it at the approximate azimuth of each of 6006 annotated MaleCNS photoreceptors. The resulting single `eye_drive` vector includes left- and right-eye cells; it is a **synthetic brightness stimulus**, not measured receptor activity or a pair of stereo images. FlyBrain adds the stimulus to receptor voltage and computes subsequent spikes. The packaged azimuths have no vertical counterpart, so receptors at the same horizontal position share a value even if their real receptive fields would differ in elevation. The video trace is a binned display of this input vector, not a spike trace.

`chase-retina` supplies successive FPV frames through this input while the detected tag bearing still stimulates `LC10a`; the speed rule and quad stabilizer remain external. `wing-direct --retina` instead omits the synthetic `LC10a` bearing stimulus. Neither mode reconstructs compound-eye optics, phototransduction, or verified T4/T5 motion tuning. See the [paired retinal pursuit experiment](../runs/chase-retina/report.md).

## Current sensor fusion state

| Quantity | Source and units | Current destination |
|---|---|---|
| RGB and tag bearing | One 320×180 FPV camera; tag angle in radians | `LC10a` in pursuit modes; 40 ms luminance frames also reach photoreceptors in `chase-retina` |
| Relative depth | Depth Anything V2 Small on the same RGB frame; not metres | Depth-weighted looming and near/far motion proxies to `LPLC2`/`LC15` only in `chase-vision`'s `flow_depth` mode; display only in `chase-retina` |
| Optical flow | RAFT Small on successive decision RGB frames; **pixels between frames**, not m/s | Coarse left/right directional drive to `T4/T5` in `flow`/`flow_depth` modes; display only in the retinal condition of `chase-retina` |
| Body angular rates | MuJoCo roll/pitch/yaw rates in rad/s | Conventional stabilizer in pursuit; heuristic `SApp08` input only with `wing-direct --gyro-3d` |
| Body linear velocity | MuJoCo state in m/s | Conventional stabilizer; not fed to MaleCNS in pursuit |
| Virtual food odor | Synthetic moving-source plume, two concentrations `0…1` at body-left/right antennas | Corresponding `ORN_DM1/ORN_VA2` populations only in `chase-odor` |

Optical flow is visual motion and mixes camera self-motion with target motion. The body-rate vector is internal rotational feedback, analogous to the fly's [haltere-based angular-velocity sensing](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1692594/), rather than a second form of optical flow. In `wing-direct --gyro-3d --neural-vision`, visual and body-rate stimuli can enter the same graph, where their injected currents propagate through existing connections; this mapping is heuristic and its biological tuning is unverified. The pursuit readout is trained on tag bearing alone and then held fixed. There is currently no explicit compensation of optical flow for measured body rotation, no calibrated RGB/depth/flow/gyro fusion, and no jointly trained readout for these inputs.

`chase-odor` adds an independent olfactory probe. The synthetic concentration is mapped to extra voltage in bilateral `ORN_DM1/ORN_VA2`; neither RGB nor depth/flow is converted to smell, and the readout is still trained on visual bearing. The odor-only condition excludes tag bearing from neural stimulation and uses fixed forward speed, while the conventional quad stabilizer remains. Paired flights gave 2/3, 1/3 and 0/3 tracking successes for tag, tag plus odor and odor only. This tests whether this model interface changes action, not whether the fly recognizes the cow as food or can naturally follow its scent. See [protocol and full traces](../runs/chase-odor/report.md).

`chase-odor-nav` replaces the visually trained `DNa02` readout with a calibrated left-minus-right ORN spike readout and an idealized upwind bearing. On 44 held-out synthetic-dose probes, ORN sign was correct on all 40 nonzero-difference cases; the four selected DM1/VA2 projection cells gave a matching left/right spike-count sign in only 9/40 (27 ties). Wind plus this receptor readout localized 4/8 new stationary sources within 0.75 m versus 0/8 for wind alone or side-swapped input, while direct ideal concentrations reached 6/8. All moving-target pursuit conditions remained 0/3. This establishes a usable receptor-level sensor interface in this simulator, not a restored innate olfactory flight circuit or a connectome advantage. [Full protocol and traces](../runs/chase-odor-navigation/report.md).

`chase-odor-fusion` holds the visual DNa02 controller fixed and masks tag measurements for 15/45 decisions in each of three moving-target flights. The fallback uses wind alone, wind plus raw odor, or wind plus calibrated ORN activity. Their pursuit successes were 1/3, 0/3 and 0/3; masked vision without fallback was 0/3, while side-swapped neural odor scored 2/3. In the wind-only trajectories, the raw odor side difference matched target-bearing sign in only 16/38 informative masked decisions. This is a negative result for the current instantaneous gradient-to-yaw rule, not evidence that natural odor laterality is reversed. The visual and odor readouts run in separate model instances, and the fusion rule is external. [Paired traces and videos](../runs/chase-odor-fusion/report.md).

The original Shiu code has also been executed separately with its version 630 FlyWire data via `scripts/shiu_smoke.py`: 127,400 neurons, 14,687,178 synapse rows, and 14 spikes in a 100 ms single-neuron stimulation smoke test. Its receipt is `runs/shiu-smoke/results.json`. This confirms that the research implementation runs locally; it does **not** test behavior or connect it to the quad. To use Shiu-like dynamics on MaleCNS would require a new parameterized model and validation against neural or behavioral observations; swapping its FlyWire matrix alone would not reproduce the published result.

An open-loop `SApp08 → b1/b2` probe (12 seeds, 100 ticks per condition) found the opposite of the expected steering-bias signs on average: left stimulus changed left-minus-right b1/b2 count by −0.67 spikes relative to rest; right stimulus changed it by +0.33. No seed had both expected signs (`runs/wing-reflex-probe/results.json`). These small differences do not establish a real reflex in either direction.

## Premotor roll pathway probe

The MaleCNS signed chemical-synapse matrix has a candidate downstream lever in the **ventral nerve cord**: two `IN08B051_d` cells per side connect to ipsilateral `i1 MN` with aggregate normalized weights +0.0287 (left) and +0.0304 (right), and also to `i2 MN`. In `flybrain`, left versus right stimulation changes the current wing adapter's roll-sign proxy in opposite directions. At extra voltage 0.5 per 20 ms tick, this orientation was found on six discovery seeds and held on five of six new seeds; mean held-out changes over 30 ticks were −1.33 for left stimulation and +2.50 for right stimulation. At 0.25, only three of six held-out seeds had both expected signs. The [paired spike records](../runs/wing-premotor-probe/dose-0p5.json) include every motor count and seed. Stronger stimulation (1.0) was also tested, but drove nearly every candidate cell on every tick.

The existing `SApp08` input does **not** directly connect to `IN08B051_d` in this matrix; its signed two-synapse aggregate is small and negative. `SApp08` does connect to `w-cHIN`, but direct `w-cHIN → i1/i2 MN` links are absent here. Five nearby `SNpp` sensory groups with direct input to `IN08B051_d` were stimulated separately; none gave reliable left/right roll signs over six held-out seeds ([sensory probe](../runs/wing-premotor-probe/sensory-candidates.json)). These statements concern the model matrix and test doses, not proof that the living fly lacks a pathway. [MANC circuit analysis](https://elifesciences.org/articles/96084) notes that many putative electrical flight-control links cannot be read from the chemical-synapse connectome.

The sensory groups also have direct positive signed edges to the candidate relay. For left `SNpp26/27/37/38/06 → IN08B051_d`, summed normalized weights are +0.0333/+0.0323/+0.0892/+0.0466/+0.0274; on the right they are +0.0279/+0.0426/+0.0361/+0.0326/+0.0337. `SNpp37/38/06` also contact ipsilateral `i2 MN`, so the relay is not the only possible route. Stimulating all five groups together at 0.5 changed the i1/i2 roll proxy with opposite left/right signs on five of six held-out seeds ([bundle probe](../runs/wing-premotor-probe/sensory-bundle.json)). The reduced `SNpp37/38/06` trio did so on six of six new held-out seeds, whereas `SNpp37/38` and `SNpp26/27` alone gave one and zero of six ([ablation probe](../runs/wing-premotor-probe/sensory-ablation.json)). A [peripheral anatomy study](https://elifesciences.org/articles/107867/figures) identifies `SNpp37/38` as tegula wing campaniform sensilla and `SNpp06/26` as distal wing campaniform sensilla; it lists `SNpp27` under unknown peripheral origin. Feeding quad angular rate into this package is an artificial cross-sensor mapping, not a reconstructed haltere reflex.

A paired intervention silenced ipsilateral `IN08B051_d` during five-group stimulation. The relay then emitted zero spikes, but the sensory effect remained at −1.42 versus −2.25 motor-spike proxy units on the left, and +1.25 versus +2.08 on the right across 12 seeds ([relay mediation](../runs/wing-premotor-probe/relay-mediation.json)). This supports partial contribution in this model; direct sensory-to-motor edges and other intermediates remain. The intervention did not silence contralateral relays, so it is not a complete pathway dissection.

For a **head-to-wing** entry, a prominent same-side descending input to `IN08B051_d` in this matrix is inhibitory `DNg08_a,DNg08_b` (summed weights −0.0231 left, −0.0247 right); `DNpe021` is weaker and positive (+0.00246/+0.00382). Stimulation of `DNp07`, `DNpe021`, `DNg08_a,DNg08_b`, and `DNg24` separately at 0.5 produced no consistent opposite-sign i1/i2 roll response on six held-out seeds each ([first three](../runs/wing-premotor-probe/descending-candidates.json), [DNg24](../runs/wing-premotor-probe/descending-dng24.json)). A usable head-to-wing path is therefore still unresolved in this model. These tests did not exhaust all descending cells or doses.

The `--premotor-roll` mode uses the quad's roll rate to choose **direct** stimulation of the opposite-side `IN08B051_d`; it does not claim the fly learned this mapping. With 100 ms decisions and the same external altitude correction, eight paired 4 s MuJoCo roll perturbations yielded 7/8 survival for this orientation, 0/8 with no stimulation, and 0/8 with sides reversed. Mean time-normalized absolute roll was 0.297, 0.543, and 0.488 rad, respectively ([full perturbation traces](../runs/wing-premotor-probe/roll-perturbations.json)). A moving-cow flight without altitude correction still crashed at 4.62 s; with external altitude correction it crashed at 7.70 s. This is evidence for a useful simulated premotor handle, not independent or biologically validated flight control.

The five-group `--sensory-roll` input used the same external gyro-to-side rule and altitude assist in matched perturbations. It survived 8/8 for 4 s with mean time-normalized absolute roll 0.247 rad; reversed sides survived 0/8 ([sensory perturbations](../runs/wing-premotor-probe/roll-perturbations-sensory.json)). In the moving-cow scene without altitude assist it still crashed at 3.03 s after losing height ([full flight](../runs/wing-sensory-flight/results.json)). These outcomes isolate simulated roll correction; they do not show autonomous sensing or full flight stability.

With only `SNpp37/38/06` (`--sensory-triplet`), the same eight perturbations yielded 7/8 survival and 0/8 with sides reversed ([triplet perturbations](../runs/wing-premotor-probe/roll-perturbations-triplet.json)). The five-group bundle remains the more reliable tested input on this small scenario set. The triplet crashed at 2.65 s in the unassisted moving-cow scene, with large roll and falling height ([triplet flight](../runs/wing-sensory-triplet/results.json)). All perturbation tests use external altitude correction and a hand-designed motor readout; none validates the fly's actual muscle-to-rotor conversion.

## Automated stochastic stimulation search

`scripts/evolve_stimulation.py` performs checkpointed mixed differential evolution over 22 named cell groups, excitation versus suppression, left/right sign rule, gain, and pulse duration. The graph and wing-to-rotor map stay fixed. Ten generations with a population of 24 produced 264 candidate evaluations and 2,112 training neural runs on four seeds. Each run resets the **same** MaleCNS graph with stochastic model noise; these are not 2,112 biological flies. Neural selection uses the paired i1/i2 roll proxy; 12 new seeds then test the finalists, four MuJoCo starts select a winner, and eight further starts provide a physical test ([report](../runs/neural-stimulation-search/report.md), [full receipt](../runs/neural-stimulation-search/results.json)).

The strongest neural candidate remained the initial `SNpp37/38/06` anchor (12/12 opposite-sign pairs on new seeds). The physics selection instead chose the five-group `SNpp26/27/37/38/06` package with gain 1.3 and almost full pulse duty. On eight new four-second perturbations with identical altitude assist, this variant survived 8/8, the fixed five-group reference survived 8/8, no stimulation survived 1/8, and reversed-side stimulation survived 0/8. The evolved variant had lower time-normalized absolute roll on 6/8 paired starts, but the small test does not establish a reliable gain over the fixed reference. In the moving-target scene without altitude assist it crashed at 3.06 s. The search therefore supports a simulated roll-feedback route, not full autonomous flight or a newly discovered head-to-wing pathway.

This search scored only `i1/i2` roll asymmetry. It therefore could not detect a population whose main effect is **power** (`DLM/DVM`) or steering through `b1/b3`. Published experiments identify `DNg02` as a flight-power regulator ([Namiki et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9206711/)), while a [MANC circuit analysis](https://elifesciences.org/articles/96084/figures) proposes `DNa04/05 → w-cHIN → b1/b3` steering routes. The latter analysis also flags potential electrical connections missing from a chemical-only graph. `scripts/probe_known_flight_circuits.py` tests these biologically directed hypotheses with separate motor readouts.

The [directed probe](../runs/directed-flight-circuits/report.md) compared 126 input/side/dose/duty configurations on six discovery and six held-out model-noise seeds. Bilateral `DNg02` stimulation increased DLM/DVM power-MN activity by a mean 90.7 spikes per 0.6 s on held-out paired seeds (14.7 at rest, 105.3 stimulated). Bilateral `SApp08` increased `w-cHIN` by 52.8 spikes, but did not change b1/b3 left-right asymmetry. `DNa04` increased `w-cHIN` by 8.8 spikes with no stable b1/b3 asymmetry, and `DNa05` also lacked a stable b1/b3 response. Left `SNpp37/38` produced a consistent i1/i2 asymmetry across six held-out seeds. These are *model* responses under synthetic constant-voltage stimulation, not demonstrated natural sensory reflexes or drone control. The `DNg02` query selected 29 cells and `SApp08` selected 47; these are population interventions, not identified single-cell mechanisms. A VFB annotation identifies one `SApp08` haltere cell in an older MaleCNS release; it does not establish the identity of every selected cell in this model.

`scripts/evolve_wing_interface.py` searches five interface scalars using 5 generations of 8 candidates, two roll-rate starts for training, and four held-out starts. On held-out starts the best candidate crashed in all four within 0.49–0.69 s; the untuned interface also crashed in all four. A conventional altitude and attitude PD baseline on the same MuJoCo quad survived the full 3 s in all four cases. The score and every candidate/episode are in `runs/wing-evolution/results.json`; the PD receipt is `runs/wing-evolution/classical_pd.json`. This is a failed small search over an unvalidated interface, not evidence that a fly brain lacks flight-stabilizing circuits.
The selected candidate raised power gain to 1.38 and reduced haltere input gain to 0.09, so the search did not find a useful sensory reflex.
The PD baseline updates at the 5 ms physics step while the neural interface updates every 100 ms; it demonstrates that the quad and disturbances are controllable, not an equal-budget neural comparison.
